Inherited House Taxes: What Heirs Usually Pay

Income tax, capital gains, property taxes, and paying the bills while probate runs.

Published September 10, 2026 · Updated October 9, 2026

Inheriting a house raises a common question: do you pay taxes on an inherited house? In most U.S. situations, inheriting the property itself is not treated like ordinary income — but property taxes, capital gains on a later sale, and estate issues still matter. This is a plain-English overview for heirs; it is not tax advice.

Usually not income tax when you inherit

For most heirs, receiving a house through probate or a will does not create immediate income tax on the inheritance itself. What often matters later is capital gains if you sell — and California property taxes under Prop 13 / Prop 19 rules when ownership changes.

Stepped-up basis (why the sale tax can be lower)

Many inherited properties receive a “step-up” in basis to fair market value around the date of death. That can reduce capital gains if heirs sell soon after. Rules and exceptions exist — talk to a CPA or tax attorney for your facts.

Property taxes still come due

County property taxes, insurance, and HOA dues do not pause for probate. Someone has to keep the house current while the case is open. That carrying cost is a major reason heirs look for cash before distribution. See California probate timelines.

Estate / inheritance taxes

Federal estate tax only hits very large estates under current exemptions. California does not currently impose a state inheritance tax like some other states. Always confirm with a professional — thresholds change.

Need cash for taxes or upkeep while you wait?

An inheritance advance can fund property taxes, insurance, or living costs without a loan payment. It is settled from the estate later, not from your paycheck.

Bottom line: Inheriting a house is rarely a simple “tax-free windfall day one.” Plan for carrying costs — and get liquidity if the wait is the real problem.
