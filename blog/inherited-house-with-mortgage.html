Inherited House With a Mortgage: What Heirs Must Do

Who pays the loan, what the lender can and cannot do, and how California heirs keep the house protected while probate runs.

Published October 27, 2026

Inheriting a home is rarely as simple as getting the keys. If the house still has a loan on it, you inherit the property and the mortgage attached to it. The good news is that you do not have to pay it off right away, and federal law gives heirs real protection. The risk is letting payments slip while probate drags on. Here is what to do with an inherited house with a mortgage in California.

The mortgage stays with the house, not with you

When someone dies, their mortgage is not forgiven. The loan remains secured by the home, and the lender keeps its right to be paid. What changes is who is responsible:

  • The estate handles the debt during probate. The executor or administrator can use estate funds to keep the loan current.
  • You are not personally liable for the loan just because you are an heir, unless you co-signed it or were a co-borrower.
  • The house is still at risk. If payments stop, the lender can eventually foreclose, and that wipes out equity that would otherwise go to the heirs.

California law also matters here. Under Probate Code section 21131, a house left to someone in a will generally passes subject to the mortgage. In other words, the heir takes the home with the loan still on it, and the rest of the estate does not have to pay it off first unless the will says otherwise.

Can the lender demand full payment when the owner dies?

Most mortgages have a due-on-sale clause that lets the lender call the loan due when the property changes hands. The federal Garn-St Germain Act blocks lenders from enforcing that clause when a home passes to a relative because the borrower died. It covers residential property with fewer than five units, which includes most family homes. That means a child, spouse, or other relative who inherits the house can usually keep the existing loan in place instead of refinancing on the lender's timeline.

Federal mortgage servicing rules add another layer. Once the servicer confirms you as a successor in interest, you can get information about the loan and be considered for options like a loan modification, even if your name was never on the note. Send the servicer a copy of the death certificate and proof of your relationship or role in the estate as early as you can.

First 30 days checklist: find the latest mortgage statement, call the servicer to report the death, ask what documents they need to confirm you as a successor in interest, confirm the homeowner's insurance is still active, and make sure someone is responsible for the next payment. Missing payments early is the most common way heirs lose equity.

Your options with an inherited mortgage

Once the immediate payments are covered, heirs typically choose one of four paths:

  1. Keep the house and the existing loan. Continue making payments on the current mortgage. This often works well if the interest rate is lower than today's rates.
  2. Refinance into your own name. Useful if you want to pull out cash, buy out other heirs, or change the loan terms. You will need to qualify on your own income and credit.
  3. Sell the house and pay off the loan. The mortgage is paid from the sale proceeds, and the remaining equity goes to the estate or heirs. In California, a sale during probate goes through the executor and the court process.
  4. Let the lender take it. If the home is worth less than the loan, heirs sometimes walk away. Talk to an attorney before choosing this, because it affects the whole estate.

When siblings inherit a house with a mortgage

Shared inheritances get complicated fast. If three siblings inherit one house, all three share the equity, but someone still has to make the monthly payment. Common arrangements include one sibling living in the home and covering the payment, the siblings splitting the cost until a sale, or one sibling refinancing to buy out the others. Put any agreement in writing, including who gets credit for payments made during probate. If you cannot agree, see our guide on what happens when heirs disagree about selling.

Reverse mortgages work differently

If the owner had a reverse mortgage, the loan usually becomes due when the last borrower dies. For HUD-insured reverse mortgages (HECMs), heirs who want to keep the home can generally pay the lesser of the full loan balance or 95 percent of the home's current appraised value. Heirs who do not want the home can sell it, and if it sells for less than the balance, they typically do not owe the difference. The servicer sets short deadlines for responding, so contact them right away.

Covering payments while probate is open

The hardest part for many families is timing. California probate often takes 9 to 18 months, and the mortgage, property taxes, insurance, and utilities keep coming the entire time. If the estate has cash, the executor can use it. If it does not, the heirs often end up paying out of pocket to protect the home. Inherited house with a mortgage FAQ

Do I have to pay the mortgage on a house I inherited?

The debt stays with the house, not with you personally, unless you co-signed the loan. But if no one makes the payments, the lender can foreclose, so the estate or the heirs usually keep paying to protect the home's equity.

Can the lender call the loan due when the owner dies?

Generally no, when the home passes to a relative. The federal Garn-St Germain Act bars lenders from enforcing a due-on-sale clause on a transfer to a relative after the borrower's death for homes with fewer than five units.

Can I take over the mortgage on an inherited house?

Often, yes. Federal servicing rules give a confirmed successor in interest the right to account information and to be considered for loss mitigation, and many heirs simply keep making the existing payments. Some heirs refinance instead.

What happens to a reverse mortgage when the owner dies?

A reverse mortgage usually becomes due when the last borrower dies. For HUD-insured loans, heirs who want to keep the home can generally pay the lesser of the loan balance or 95 percent of the current appraised value. Contact the servicer quickly because deadlines are short.

Can an inheritance advance help with mortgage payments during probate?

Yes. An inheritance advance gives you cash from your share of the estate before probate closes. It is not a loan, has no monthly payment, and requires no credit check, so some heirs use it to cover their part of the mortgage, taxes, or insurance while they wait.
