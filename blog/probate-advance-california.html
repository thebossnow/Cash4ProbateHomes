Probate Advance in California: Costs, Timing, and Eligibility

What a probate cash advance really costs, how long it takes, and who qualifies when a California estate is still open.

Published October 13, 2026

California probate is slow. Most estates take 9 to 18 months to close, and some take longer. Meanwhile the bills keep coming: property taxes, insurance, utilities, a mortgage on the inherited house, or your own expenses that cannot wait. A probate advance lets a named heir turn part of a future inheritance into cash now, while the estate keeps moving through court. This guide explains what it costs, how long it takes, and who qualifies in California.

What is a probate advance?

A probate advance (also called a probate cash advance or inheritance advance) is a lump-sum payment to an heir or beneficiary in exchange for an assignment of part of that person's share of the estate. When the estate distributes, the agreed amount is paid to the funding company directly from your share. You do not make monthly payments and you do not pay it back out of your own pocket.

It is not a loan. There is no interest rate, no credit check, and no collateral. At Cash4ProbateHomes, the advance is non-recourse: if your inheritance turns out to be smaller than expected because of creditor claims, falling property values, or a legal challenge, that risk sits with us, not you. That is the main difference from a probate loan, which is debt you owe no matter how the estate turns out.

How much does a probate advance cost?

Instead of interest, a probate advance uses a fixed fee that is agreed before you sign. The fee is the difference between the cash you receive today and the portion of your inheritance that goes to the funder at distribution. Because the funder is taking on the risk and waiting for probate to finish, the fee depends on your specific case. The main factors are:

  • The size of your share. Your advance is based on the value of what you stand to inherit, not your income or credit.
  • Estate debts and liens. Mortgages, tax liens, and creditor claims reduce what is left for heirs.
  • How far along the case is. An estate with letters already issued and a clear inventory is easier to evaluate than one that has just been filed.
  • Complications. A will contest, disputes between heirs, or unclear title adds risk and time.

When you compare offers, look at the total amount that will come out of your inheritance, not just the check you receive. Ask whether the fee is fixed or whether it grows the longer probate takes, since some providers use fees that step up over time. Our fee is fixed up front, so a slow court does not cost you more. We also charge no application fee and no upfront fees.

California protection for heirs: Under California Probate Code section 11604, the probate court can review an assignment of an heir's inheritance before ordering distribution, and it can refuse to enforce an agreement whose terms it finds grossly unreasonable. Any reputable funder expects its agreement to stand up to that review.

How long does it take to get a probate advance?

The advance moves on its own timeline, separate from the court. Here is how it typically works with us:

  1. Apply online in about 5 minutes. Share your contact details, the inherited property, and what you know about the probate case.
  2. Case review and verification. We confirm that you are a named heir and review the case documents and property value. This step is typically completed within 24 to 48 hours.
  3. Offer and agreement. You see the advance amount and the fixed amount that will be settled from your share. There is no obligation to accept.
  4. Funding. Once eligibility is confirmed and you accept, funds can arrive in as little as 24 hours.
  5. Settlement at distribution. When the estate closes, the agreed amount is paid from your share. You never make a payment yourself.

The biggest variable is paperwork. If the case number, the personal representative's name, and the property address are easy to verify, things move faster. You can apply with what you have, and we can help fill in the gaps.

Who is eligible for a probate advance in California?

You will generally qualify if all of these are true:

  • You are a named heir or beneficiary of the estate, either under a will or under California's intestate succession rules if there is no will.
  • The probate case is in California. You can live anywhere; only the case needs to be here.
  • The estate includes real estate or other assets with enough value, after debts, to support your share.

Bad credit, no job, or being retired do not disqualify you. Eligibility is about the inheritance, not your finances. You also do not need permission from the executor or the other heirs to advance against your own share, because you are only assigning part of what is yours.

When does a probate advance make sense?

An advance is worth considering when you need cash before the estate closes and you do not want to take on debt or force a quick sale. Common reasons include keeping up with the mortgage, property taxes, or insurance on an inherited home; paying funeral or legal costs; covering moving or repair costs; or handling personal bills while you wait. California's creditor claim period alone runs at least four months after the court issues letters, so even simple estates rarely distribute quickly.

An advance may not be the right fit if the estate is about to close anyway, if your share is very small, or if the estate's own funds can cover the carrying costs. It is also worth comparing other options, such as a family allowance from the court, which we cover in how to get money before probate closes.

Probate advance FAQ

Is a probate cash advance a loan?

No. It is an assignment of part of your inheritance. There is no interest, no monthly payment, and no credit check, and it is settled from the estate when it distributes.

Do I have to pay it back if the estate is worth less than expected?

No. Our advances are non-recourse. If creditor claims, lower property values, or legal challenges shrink your inheritance, that risk is ours. Read more in do you have to pay back an inheritance advance.

Does the executor have to approve my advance?

No. You are assigning part of your own share, so you do not need the executor's or the other heirs' approval. The assignment is handled through the estate when it distributes.

How fast can I get the money?

Case review is typically completed within 24 to 48 hours, and once eligibility is confirmed, funds can be available in as little as 24 hours.

Can I get an advance if I live outside California?

Yes. What matters is that the probate case is in California. Your own location has no effect on eligibility.
