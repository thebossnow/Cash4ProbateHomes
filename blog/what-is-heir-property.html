What Is Heir Property?

Why shared inherited real estate gets stuck — and how heirs can still access cash.

Heir property usually means real estate passed to heirs — often without a clear, recorded title plan — so ownership is shared, cloudy, or stuck in probate. If you inherited a share of a house and cannot freely sell or refinance, you may be dealing with heir property (or a probate-held home with the same practical problems).

Why heir property is hard to deal with

  • Multiple heirs each own a fraction — decisions need agreement or court help
  • Title may still be in the decedent's name until probate finishes
  • Banks and buyers often will not touch unclear title
  • One heir may live in the home while others need cash
  • Taxes and insurance still come due even when title is messy

Heir property vs. a normal probate house

In a straightforward California probate, the court process eventually clears title so the personal representative can sell or distribute the home. “Heir property” problems show up when heirs inherit without that clean path — or when probate stalls and everyone is stuck waiting. Either way, heirs often need money before title is clean.

Options when you own a share

  1. Wait for probate / title clearing — safest for title, slow for cash.
  2. Agree with co-heirs to sell — works if everyone cooperates; see heir disagreements.
  3. Buy out siblings — possible later, hard without liquidity now.
  4. Inheritance advance on your share — access part of your expected inheritance without forcing a sale. Learn how an inheritance advance works.
Bottom line: You do not always have to wait for a clean sale to get cash from your share. If you are a named heir with property in the estate, an advance can bridge the gap while title and probate catch up.
