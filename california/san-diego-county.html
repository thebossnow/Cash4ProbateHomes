Where San Diego County probate cases are handled

Probate in San Diego County is handled by the Probate Division of the Superior Court of California, County of San Diego. Estate cases are heard at the Central Courthouse, 1100 Union Street, San Diego, CA 92101, in four probate departments (501 to 504).

The Probate Business Office is on the third floor of the Central Courthouse. It is open 8:30 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., Monday to Friday, except court holidays. Phone: (619) 844-2676 (answered 8:30 to 11:30 a.m.). In North County, the North County Regional Center at 325 South Melrose Drive in Vista has a probate drop box only. Mail goes to the Central Courthouse.

Attorneys must file probate papers electronically (required since April 15, 2021). People filing without a lawyer do not have to e-file, but the court encourages it.

How a San Diego County probate case moves

The court's own timeline (form PR-195) says a decedent's estate takes about 9 months to 1.5 years, or longer. The main steps:

  • Within 30 days of death, whoever has the original will must deliver it to the court clerk.
  • File the Petition for Probate (DE-111). Publish and serve notice at least 15 days before the hearing.
  • About 2 weeks before each hearing, check the Probate Examiner Notes on the court's online Register of Actions. Any "NEED" items must be fixed by filing at least 5 court days before the hearing.
  • Within 4 months after Letters are issued, send the inventory to the Probate Referee and give notice to creditors.
  • Within 1 to 1.5 years after Letters, file the final accounting (or waiver) and petition for final distribution. Heirs usually get their share after this step.

If the house is sold during probate, the sale may need court confirmation. Each San Diego probate department sets aside a weekly "Sales" calendar for these hearings. During the wait, the inherited home still has property taxes, insurance, mortgage payments, and upkeep to cover. An inheritance advance bridges that gap.

Smaller estates may skip full probate

The court's self-help guide (PR-193) says formal probate is common when the estate is worth more than $208,850. Simpler options can apply:

  • Personal property worth $208,850 or less: Affidavit for Collection of Property without Probate (local form PR-132). This cannot be used for real estate.
  • A primary residence where the estate is $750,000 or less: Petition to Determine Succession to Primary Residence (DE-310).

These limits can change and depend on the date of death, so check with the court or a lawyer.

Property tax steps for San Diego County heirs

  • Tell the Assessor within 150 days of the death. File the Change in Ownership Statement, Death of Real Property Owner (BOE-502-D), even if the home was in a trust. Late filing can bring a penalty. Mail to San Diego County Assessor, P.O. Box 121750, San Diego, CA 92112-1750, or file at any Assessor office.
  • Keep paying the tax bill. San Diego County's first installment is due Nov. 1 (late after Dec. 10). The second is due Feb. 1 (late after April 10). Late payments get a 10% penalty.
  • Prop 19 parent-child exclusion. Children or grandchildren who move in may keep the parent's lower tax base. The home must have been the parent's main home and become the child's main home within 1 year. File a Homeowners' Exemption within 1 year and the Parent-Child Exclusion claim (BOE-19-P) within 3 years, or before selling to someone else, whichever comes first. See the Assessor's Prop 19 page.
  • Death certificates. Certified death certificates and Affidavits of Death are handled by the San Diego Recorder/County Clerk, in person, by mail, or online.

Sources: San Diego Superior Court Probate and San Diego County Assessor/Recorder/County Clerk. Rules and limits can change, so confirm with the court before filing. Last updated November 2026.

San Diego County Inheritance Advance FAQ

How long does probate take in San Diego County?

The court's own timeline (form PR-195) says a San Diego County estate usually takes about 9 months to 1.5 years, and longer if the estate is contested or a home has to be sold. Heirs typically cannot receive their share until the case is nearly complete, which is why many San Diego County heirs use an inheritance advance to access cash sooner.

Which court handles probate in San Diego County?

The Probate Division of the San Diego County Superior Court handles probate. Estate cases are heard at the Central Courthouse, 1100 Union Street, San Diego, CA 92101, and the Probate Business Office is on the third floor.

Do I have to live in San Diego County to get an inheritance advance?

No. Your location does not affect eligibility. As long as the probate estate is in San Diego County, or anywhere in California, you can qualify, even if you live in another county or state. We serve heirs in San Diego, Chula Vista, Oceanside and throughout the county.

How much does an inheritance advance cost in San Diego County?

The cost is a fixed amount agreed up front based on the value of your inheritance, not your credit or where you live. There are no monthly payments and no interest, and San Diego County heirs pay nothing out of pocket. The advance is settled from the estate when probate closes.

Where do I file probate in North County?

Estate cases are heard at the Central Courthouse in downtown San Diego. The North County Regional Center in Vista has a probate drop box only.

How do I check if my San Diego probate petition has problems?

Look up your case on the court's online Register of Actions and read the Probate Examiner Notes. Fix any "NEED" items at least 5 court days before the hearing.

Do I have to tell the San Diego County Assessor when a homeowner dies?

Yes. File form BOE-502-D within 150 days of the death, even if the home was in a trust.

Can I keep my parent's low property tax on an inherited San Diego home?

Possibly, if you move in. Under Prop 19 you must make it your main home within 1 year, file a Homeowners' Exemption within 1 year, and file the BOE-19-P claim within 3 years.
